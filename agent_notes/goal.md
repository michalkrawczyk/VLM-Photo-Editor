# Goal

Existing tools force a tradeoff: full manual control (Photoshop) or single-click automation with no visibility into what changed — e.g. Luminar's one-click background swap shifts skin tone with no way to undo just that step. This app closes that gap: a photo app that edits backgrounds, frames photos, applies small corrections (exposure, white balance, denoise — not full grading), and learns the user's editing style over time once the user opts in — with a plan you can see and steer in plain language before execution in `manual`/`auto_low`/`auto_medium`, or immediately after via the replayable recipe in `full_auto`. A VLM proposes a concise human-readable plan first; on accept, an executor compiles it into deterministic, validated operations. Background edits may use genAI; everything else uses Python imaging libs. Non-destructive throughout.

Four accept modes (`AcceptMode` enum): `manual` — human approves every plan; `auto_low` — skips approval when all ops are LOW risk; `auto_medium` — skips approval when all ops are LOW or MEDIUM risk; `full_auto` — executes immediately after compile, no pre-execution human gate, including genAI background ops. Safe because subject pixels are frozen and saves are always non-destructive: a bad result costs a rerun, not damage. `full_auto` still runs under a per-run ceiling (max genAI generations / spend); in batch it never finalises silently — a mandatory post-run review surface lists every auto-executed image for accept / revert / re-plan before "Save All".

Batch/bulk processing across multiple photos is in scope: images plan concurrently, but each image's execution stays gated by the active `AcceptMode` — manual review per image unless an `auto_*` or `full_auto` mode already permits skipping it. Batch parallelizes planning; it never grants approval that single-image mode wouldn't. A per-image failure (plan or execution) surfaces against that image only, in its own row of the batch review queue — it never halts, retries indefinitely, or silently drops sibling images. The batch review queue is the mandatory gate for `full_auto` too: images are auto-executed but not treated as final until the user has seen the post-run review.
